WHERE TO LOOK IN THE REAL FILES
==============================================================================

Case: Baze v. Rees  553 U.S. 35

1. THE DATASET RECORD
   File: data\dataset\uslegalqa_v3_final.jsonl
   Line: 3644
   In VS Code, open the file, press Ctrl+G, type the line number.
   That line is the complete record, including span_start and
   span_end. The same line is copied to 01_raw_dataset_line.txt and
   pretty printed in 02_pair_record.json.

2. THE OPINION TEXT
   File: data/cleaned/opinions.jsonl
   Line: 215
   This line holds the full text of the opinion as a JSON string.
   Because it is one long line, character offsets into the text are
   not the same as editor columns, which is why the marked copy in
   03_opinion_marked.txt exists.

3. THE SPAN IN PLACE
   Open 03_opinion_marked.txt and search for:
       SUPPORTING SPAN BEGINS
   The text between the two markers is exactly the characters at
   positions 1525 to 1791 of the opinion.

4. THE PROOF
   Open 04_proof.txt. It shows the stored span and the slice taken
   from the opinion at those offsets, and whether they are identical.
   Result for this pair: IDENTICAL = True

5. TO CHECK WITHOUT ANY OF THIS TOOLING
   python -c "import json;d=[json.loads(l) for l in open('data/dataset/uslegalqa_v3_final.jsonl',encoding='utf-8')];p=d[3643];o=[json.loads(l) for l in open('data/cleaned/opinions.jsonl',encoding='utf-8')];t=[x for x in o if x['cluster_id']==p['cluster_id']][0]['text'];print(t[p['span_start']:p['span_end']]==p['supporting_span'])"
